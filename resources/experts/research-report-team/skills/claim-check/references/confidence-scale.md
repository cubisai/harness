# Confidence Scale Reference

Grade each checked claim with exactly one level. The level measures agreement between checked sources — not how confident the sentence sounds.

## Levels

**High** — Three or more independent sources agree, or one primary or official source states the claim exactly, in the right period and scope.

**Medium** — Two independent sources agree, or one strong secondary source reports it and names the primary that was checked.

**Low** — A single weak source carries it, or the apparent agreement dissolves because both sources depend on the same origin (one press release, one dataset, one interview).

**Unsupported** — No checked source says it. The claim must be corrected or removed; confident wording cannot save it.

## Worked examples

1. Claim: "The agency's retail sales series rose 4.1 percent for the stated year." The statistical agency's published table states this figure → **High** (one primary, stated exactly).
2. Claim: "Most consumers prefer local shops." A single industry survey of one region says it, and no independent record repeats it → **Low** (one weak source under a sweeping scope; soften to that region or remove).
3. Claim: "The launch raised supplier prices within a year." A study and a regulator's report, independent of each other, agree → **Medium** (two independent sources); it moves to High only once a third source appears that does not trace back to either one.
