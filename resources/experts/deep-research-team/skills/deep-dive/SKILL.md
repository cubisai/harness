# Deep Dive

## Step 1 — Frame the slice
1. Write the question you own in one sentence, plus what is out of scope (what other members cover).
2. List 3–6 search angles: the plain question, its key terms and synonyms, the main players, recent changes ("2025", "2026"), and the counter-view ("criticism", "failed", "problems").

## Step 2 — Go wide, then deep
3. Skim many results before reading any closely. Prefer, in order: primary sources (official sites, filings, papers, documentation, datasets), then established press and analysts, then forums for lived experience.
4. Read deeply only the sources that answer the slice. Two good primary sources beat ten summaries of each other.
5. Track the date of every source; prefer the newest for anything that changes fast.

## Step 3 — Record findings
6. One finding per line: a single checkable sentence, then `[source title or domain, date]`.
7. Keep numbers exact: value, unit, period, population. Never round away a qualifier.
8. When sources disagree, record both and say which is more authoritative and why.

## Step 4 — Close
9. Gaps: what you looked for and could not find.
10. Leads: up to three places that would repay a deeper look.

## Output
- Save the full notes with `team_write` (name `<your-id>-<step>`), then reply to the lead with at most 200 words: the top findings, the file name and open questions.
- With no web or file access, write "from memory, unverified" on every finding.
