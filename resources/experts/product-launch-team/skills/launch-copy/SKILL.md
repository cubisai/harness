# Launch Copy

## When to use
Use once the lead hands over the agreed positioning with its proof points. Without a positioning statement, say so and stop; copy without one message drifts.

## Step 1 — Extract the message
1. Write the single message as one sentence you could say out loud, taken from the agreed positioning.
2. List the proof points the positioning supplies; the copy may claim nothing beyond them.
3. Name the one action the reader should take; every piece ends driving it.
4. List the top two objections a skeptical reader would raise and which proof answers each.

## Step 2 — Draft headline and subhead
5. Write three headline variants, each testing a distinct idea: pain, outcome, category. Pick one primary and say why in a line.
6. Write one subhead per headline. Each adds a fact or stakes the claim; none repeats the headline.
7. Keep headlines under 60 characters so search results and inboxes do not truncate them.
8. Keep all six plain language: words the audience already uses, no jargon, no superlatives the proof list does not carry.

## Step 3 — Write the pieces
9. Product page section, 150–250 words: one idea per sentence, active voice, benefits before features, each proof point placed where its claim lands.
10. Announcement post, 150–250 words: open on the reader's problem, not the company's news, and put the call to action in the final line.
11. Launch email, about 120 words: subject line under 50 characters, body skimmable in one glance, one call to action.
12. Carry the same call to action into all three pieces and print it by itself at the end of the deliverable.

## Step 4 — Check before sending
13. Read each piece aloud; cut any line that does not earn its place.
14. Run the set against [references/copy-checklist.md](references/copy-checklist.md) and fix failures in the copy rather than noting them. Read that file once for the set, not per line.
15. Mark any missing price, name or figure `[placeholder]`, never as fact.

## Do not
- Do not invent testimonials, statistics or quotes; proof comes only from the given list.
- Do not write for two audiences in one piece; the positioning names one.
- Do not pad to look thorough; a tighter line beats a longer one here.

## Output
- Save the full result (message line, headline and subhead variants, page section, post, email, call to action) with the `team_write` tool under the name `copywriter-launch-copy`.
- Reply to the lead with at most 200 words plus that file name and any open questions.
