# Source Types Reference

Where each kind of demand evidence comes from and how far it can be trusted. Grade a source before relying on it.

## Kinds and reliability

**Primary product sources** — Competitor pricing pages, docs, release notes, published terms. Reliable for what rivals offer and charge today; weak for what users actually feel. Pages change, so record the date you saw them.

**Reviews and marketplaces** — App store listings, review platforms, plugin stores. Real customer language and ratings; skewed toward strong feelings and older versions. Quote recent reviews over the average score.

**Communities and forums** — Reddit, Discord and Slack groups, niche boards, Q&A sites. Raw unmet needs and daily workarounds, often before any survey shows them; unrepresentative and noisy. One thread is an anecdote; a theme repeated across independent threads is a signal.

**Search and trend tools** — Search volume, keyword difficulty, interest curves. Show attention, not intent to buy; tools disagree. Use directionally and name the tool and period.

**Industry and press figures** — Market-size reports, funding news, coverage pieces. Good for scale and direction; press releases inflate. Trace each figure to its origin — two articles citing one study count as one source.

**First-party inputs** — Interview notes, support threads, sales objections passed in the brief. The most decision-relevant evidence available; scope is small, so say whose words each is.

## Labels

`sourced` = a record backs the item; cite its number. `assumption` = no record; name the cheapest check that would settle it.
