# Market Scan

## When to use
Use at the start of a launch, once the lead names the product and the audience. Do not use for positioning wording or channel planning; the strategist and the planner own those.

## Step 1 — Define the segment and the job
1. Restate in one line: the product, the segment the lead named, and the question this scan must answer.
2. Define the target segment precisely: who feels the problem, what triggers it, and what they can buy.
3. State the job to be done — the outcome the customer would "hire" the product for, in words the material uses.
4. Name what that customer uses today, even if it is a spreadsheet or doing nothing.
5. If the segment or goal is ambiguous, ask the lead once before searching instead of guessing.

## Step 2 — Map competitors and alternatives
6. Find 3–6 competitors or alternatives. Include at least one non-software alternative: the manual workaround or doing nothing.
7. Record one row per competitor: what it is, its stated positioning in their own words (quoted), published pricing, and the buyer it clearly serves.
8. Mark anything unverifiable — hidden pricing, dead pages — missing, not estimated.
9. When a source's reliability needs judging, read [references/source-types.md](references/source-types.md) and grade it before use. Read that file only when quality must be judged.

## Step 3 — Collect demand evidence
10. Gather evidence with sources: search and trend interest, community threads, review counts and recurring review themes, published market figures.
11. Log each item as `[n] Source — Date — What it shows`, with the exact quote or figure pasted under it.
12. Label every item `sourced` (a record backs it) or `assumption` (plausible, no record — name the cheapest way to check it).
13. Note where evidence conflicts, such as reviews praising what pricing pages undercut, and keep both sides.

## Step 4 — Summarise gaps and risks
14. Write 3–5 findings, each as: finding, supporting items by number, confidence.
15. List gaps the alternatives leave unsolved, each backed by evidence or marked an open question.
16. List risks: crowded field, thin evidence, shifting demand — each with the check that would confirm or refute it.
17. End with the one question that would most change the launch if answered.

## Do not
- Do not invent reviews, figures or competitor claims; a missing fact stays missing.
- Do not turn one anecdote into a finding; count the independent sources behind each claim.
- Do not range outside the named segment; log tangents as out of scope instead.

## Output
- Save the full result (segment and job statement, competitor table with pricing, evidence list, findings, gaps and risks) with the `team_write` tool under the name `market-researcher-market-scan`.
- Reply to the lead with at most 200 words plus that file name and any open questions.
